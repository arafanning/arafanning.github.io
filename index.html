About

Scientist-practitioner training grounded in neuropsychology

I am a doctoral candidate in Clinical Science in Child and Adolescent Psychology at Florida International University, where I work with Raul Gonzalez, Ph.D., in the Substance Use Neuropsychology Lab. I completed an M.S. in Developmental Psychology at Illinois State University and undergraduate degrees in Neuroscience and Communication Sciences and Disorders at Augustana College.

My long-term goal is to work as an academic neuropsychologist in a medical center, integrating complex clinical assessment with research that improves how cognitive functioning is measured, interpreted, and connected to patients’ daily lives.

Research

Understanding when and how substance use matters for cognitive health

My research focuses on heterogeneity: associations that differ across levels of use, developmental periods, health conditions, and functional outcomes.

01

HIV, cannabis, and neurocognition

I examine whether cannabis-related neurocognitive associations differ for people living with HIV and whether disease severity helps explain variability in cognitive and functional outcomes.

02

Adolescent substance use

Using longitudinal data, including the ABCD Study®, I investigate developmental, social, behavioral, and neurocognitive factors associated with substance-use initiation and change.

03

Measurement and quantitative methods

My work uses robust regression, nonlinear contrasts, latent-variable models, demographically adjusted scores, and careful psychometric reasoning to improve clinical interpretation.

Dissertation

Understanding the Effect of Cannabis on the Neurocognitive Health of People Living With HIV

This program of research examines neurocognition and everyday functioning as distinct but related outcomes, with particular attention to nonlinear patterns of cannabis use and the role of HIV disease burden.
